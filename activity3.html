04
Our Stand

Position Paper

Mandatory community service should be part of school policy for senior high school students.

Introduction

Mandatory community service for senior high school students has become an important issue in school policy because schools are expected not only to develop students’ academic knowledge but also to shape them into responsible and active members of society. Community service includes activities such as clean-up drives, environmental projects, and assistance in local organizations.

Schools implement mandatory community service as part of graduation requirements because it can help students develop civic engagement and social responsibility (Bennett, 2009). Research also shows that required community service can influence students’ future participation in volunteering and civic activities, especially when the experience is meaningful and supported by the school and community (Kim & Kim, 2025). Therefore, this paper argues that mandatory community service should be part of school policy for senior high school students because it develops civic responsibility, provides meaningful learning experiences outside the classroom, and encourages students to become active contributors to their communities.

01
Supporting Evidence

Arguments

Three reasons support the group’s position.

1. Develops Civic Responsibility

Schools are responsible not only for helping students succeed academically but also for preparing them to participate responsibly in society. Although students learn about social responsibility in their subjects, they do not always have opportunities to put these lessons into practice. Making community service part of school policy would give senior high school students a structured way to participate in activities that address actual community needs, such as environmental protection, educational assistance, and outreach programs. Community involvement should not depend entirely on whether students already have the motivation or opportunity to volunteer.

Lee and Cnaan (2025) examined the effects of mandatory community service for middle and high school students in South Korea. They found that the policy increased the likelihood of volunteering in adulthood among lower socioeconomic groups, although they found no comparable effect among higher socioeconomic groups. These findings suggest that mandatory service can create opportunities for future civic participation, but its effects are not guaranteed for everyone. Schools should establish community service as a meaningful educational responsibility, with activities that help students understand the needs of others rather than simply complete a required number of hours.

2. Provides Meaningful Learning Outside the Classroom

Mandatory community service can give students opportunities to apply their knowledge and develop skills in real-world situations. Organizing a donation drive, for example, requires students to plan activities, coordinate responsibilities, communicate with different people, and respond to unexpected challenges. Such experiences can complement classroom instruction by allowing students to practice cooperation, decision-making, and problem-solving outside an academic setting.

Filges et al. (2022) conducted a systematic review of service-learning among students from kindergarten to Grade 12. Their findings showed that the evidence for many academic outcomes was inconclusive, meaning that participation alone cannot be assumed to improve academic achievement. This highlights the need for schools to establish clear learning objectives and connect service activities to students’ educational experiences. Well-organized projects, with appropriate supervision and reflection, can make community service a purposeful part of school education rather than an unrelated extracurricular obligation.

3. Encourages Meaningful Community Involvement

A successful community service policy must consider whether students’ experiences encourage genuine involvement and whether activities respond to the needs of the people they are intended to help. Without proper planning, a mandatory program could become a burden for students or an activity that provides little benefit to the community. Schools should work with barangay officials, local organizations, and community members to identify suitable projects, establish realistic expectations, and ensure that students’ contributions are useful.

Cho et al. (2023), in a survey of 605 compulsory volunteers involved in sports events in Singapore, found that motivations such as community involvement, personal growth, and expressing personal values were positively associated with the intention to continue volunteering. Although this study did not examine a senior high school community service policy directly, it offers an important insight: the way people experience compulsory service may influence their willingness to participate again. Schools should give students meaningful responsibilities, explain the purpose of their activities, and consider academic workloads, accessibility, and individual circumstances. A fair, purposeful program responsive to genuine community needs can benefit both students and the communities they serve.

02
Final Recommendation

Conclusion

A purposeful, supervised program can support responsible citizenship.

In conclusion, mandatory community service should be part of school policy for senior high school students because it develops civic responsibility, offers meaningful learning experiences beyond the classroom, and encourages students to become active contributors to their communities. While community service should not be treated as a simple requirement to complete, a well-designed program can help students understand social responsibility and apply their skills to real community needs.

Therefore, schools should require senior high school students to participate in supervised, purposeful community service activities that are connected to clear learning goals. To make this policy effective, schools should coordinate with barangays and local organizations, choose projects that address genuine community needs, provide proper guidance and reflection activities, and consider students’ academic workloads and personal circumstances. Through this approach, mandatory community service can prepare students not only for graduation but also for responsible and engaged citizenship.
