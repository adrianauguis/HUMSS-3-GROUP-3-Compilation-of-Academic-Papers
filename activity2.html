Critique Paper: The Lion King

Disney’s The Lion King performance at the Olivier Awards 2019 with Mastercard

Introduction

This paper critiques the cast of The Lion King performing at the 2019 Olivier Awards. The performance brings the musical’s world to the stage through vocals, music, choreography, costumes, puppetry, and coordinated ensemble work. It will be examined through Structuralism, Feminism, and Marxism to consider how the production communicates meaning and represents relationships, gender, authority, and labor.

Performance Strengths

The production quickly captures attention with powerful opening vocals, bright costumes, animal puppets, group harmonies, and energetic choreography. The stage gradually becomes the world of The Lion King through sound, movement, and visual design. The performers show strong timing and physical commitment, making the costumes and puppets feel alive rather than decorative. Music does more than accompany the action: rhythm and percussion help determine the pace and energy of the scene. The relationship among music, movement, costumes, and ensemble patterns makes the performance feel connected and purposeful.

Structuralist Approach

From a structuralist perspective, the performance’s meaning comes from the relationships among its elements rather than from any single costume, performer, or musical cue. Animal-inspired movements become recognizable through their combination with costumes and music, while the ensemble gains meaning through coordinated group movement. These repeated patterns reinforce the story’s themes of connection, community, and the natural world.

Feminist Approach

A feminist reading recognizes that women onstage actively contribute to the performance’s energy and success rather than serving only as decoration. However, the larger story places significant emphasis on male authority and succession, particularly through Mufasa and Simba. This creates a tension between the collective effort visible in the performance and the traditional male-centered structure of leadership within the narrative.

Marxist Approach

A Marxist reading draws attention to the labor behind the spectacle. Singers and dancers are visible to the audience, but puppeteers, musicians, costume makers, choreographers, designers, technicians, and stage workers also make the production possible. Their work can become nearly invisible when the final performance appears seamless. The awards-show setting also turns the performance into a showcase of the skill, resources, and labor required for a major theatrical production.

Weaknesses and Emotional Impact

The production’s visual richness can occasionally become excessive. Costumes, puppetry, choreography, music, and a large cast compete for attention, which may cause subtle emotional moments to be overlooked. This does not ruin the performance, but it can reduce intimacy. Nevertheless, the gradual musical build, the energy of the performers, and the transformation of the stage create a celebratory and memorable atmosphere, even for viewers unfamiliar with every detail of the story.

Conclusion

The Lion King’s Olivier Awards performance succeeds because its ambition is matched by the cast’s coordination and control. Its greatest strength is the unity of music, movement, visual design, and ensemble work; its main weakness is that the scale of the spectacle can overshadow individual emotional details. Through Structuralism, Feminism, and Marxism, the performance can be understood not only as entertainment but also as a collaborative artwork that raises questions about connection, gender, power, and the often-unseen labor behind theatrical success.
